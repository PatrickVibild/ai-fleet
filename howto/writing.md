# Scientific writing

Which skill to reach for at each step of a paper. Click a skill to copy it, then paste it into a Claude session.

## 1. Find out what's known

- `/research`: a question you need answered from primary sources (papers, docs, specs). Writes the findings, with citations, to a Markdown file in the repo.
- `/teach`: you need to understand a method or field yourself before writing about it.

## 2. Sharpen the claim

- `/grill-me`: stress-test the contribution, the argument and the outline before drafting.
- `/domain-modeling`: fix the paper's terms in GLOSSARY.md, so a word means one thing from the abstract to the appendix.
- `/to-questionnaire`: a decision only your co-authors or supervisor can make. Turns it into questions they can answer.

## 3. Plan the paper

- `/to-spec`: write down the agreed outline, the claims and the evidence each section needs.
- `/to-tickets`: one ticket per section, figure or piece of tooling, marked with its kind so the right implement command runs.

## 4. Draft

- `/implement-orchestrator`: draft sections and build figures in parallel through the fleet.
- `/implement`: draft one section in this session from its ticket.
- `/prototype`: try out a figure or table layout before committing to it.

## 5. Revise

- `/humanizer`: rewrite prose that reads as AI-generated. Same claims, better voice.
- `/wait-what`: a paragraph didn't land. Has it re-pitched from a different angle.
- `/code-review`: check a section against its ticket and the paper's style rules, the same way as code.

## Housekeeping

- `/handoff`: the context is getting full. Writes a handoff document for a fresh session.
- `/retro`: after a writing session, to learn what to do differently.
